ACT 1

The Cellular Metropolis

Imagine a living cell not as a chaotic test tube of random chemical events, but as a bustling, highly organized metropolis. Individual biomolecules—including proteins, metabolites, peptides, and lipids—are the diverse "Citizens" walking the streets, each with highly specialized skills and roles.

"In a city, to attain some lofty goals, we need to bring people together. Similarly, to achieve some specific biological outcomes, proteins must come together, often at specific time and location. Thus, our lab focus on creating chimeras to bring proteins together in the cells to achieve specific biological outcome. We are in advantage position to do so as we have high efficient experimental and computational tools to map the cellular network and interactome. In addition, our derivation-free platform identify ligands that could engage these 'citizens' of the cells, where chimeras could be created to bring crucial players in this cellular metropolis to crystallise specific purpose."

Above narrative underpins our core focus on proximity biology where cellular processes are carried by protein assemblies. Rather than targeting isolated proteins, we look at the cellular metropolis as a collective system where strategic matchmaking between proteins could rewire cellular processes and drive profound therapeutic effects.

ACT 2 The Shift Evolution

From Mapping to Tinkering

Our laboratory's journey started from pioneering native bimolecular interaction profiling (Mapping Stage) to designing active proximity matchmaking molecules (Tinkering Stage).

ESTABLISHED FOUNDATION

Systematic Interactome Mapping & Mechanism-of-Action Studies

From the beginning, our laboratory has focused on dissecting the mechanisms of bioactive compounds using techniques developed or co-developed by the lab, believing that target discovery and validation are more effective starting from bioactive compounds rather than unproven targets. To this end, we invented Thermal Proximity Co-Aggregation (TPCA)[1]—a system-wide approach based on CETSA-MS or TPP to profile protein complexes directly in intact cells—and have continually improvised and deployed TPP and TPCA to dissect target-binding and downstream mechanisms, tracking global network rewiring, and identifying ligands of endogenous protein in native context without chemical derivatization. We study how chemicals perturb proteins and rewire biomolecular interaction networks leading to diseased phenotypes, and conversely, how chemicals can alter cellular behaviors for therapeutic or industrial purposes. We work closely with collaborators to expedite experimental validation, complementing our techniques with genetic approaches to accelerate the discovery of new drug targets and biology.

🔗 Why the Progression

Crucially, the dynamic interactions of proteins—both among themselves and with ligands—mapped comprehensively in the Mapping Stage is only the beginning: our ultimate aspiration is to intervene in biological processes and disease progression. This is precisely why the comprehensive interactome maps, native ligand repertoires, and mechanistic insights accumulated over years of MOA studies form the essential pilot and foundational data arsenal for our new direction. They provide the exact structural coordinates, native binding configurations, and tag-free recruitment handles that serve as the essential guide and foundational building blocks for generating proximity-inducing chimeras in the Tinkering Stage. By fully resolving the cellular metropolis's baseline connectivity, we gain an unparalleled strategic advantage: the ability to construct precision chemical matchmakers that can strategically manipulate, reprogram, or completely rewire disease-driving biology. We now stand at the threshold of a new phase—transitioning from mapping and understanding to actively tinkering with the cellular metropolis.

The Laboratory Discovery Pipeline

Our Strategy & Strengths

A clear, sequential look at how our technologies collaborate to deliver molecular medicine.

STEP 1: Deep Mapping

We use advanced thermal and proximity aggregation proteomics (Slim-TPCA, Mito-TPCA, MAPS-iTSA) to physically map protein assemblies and chemical ligands that could serve as recruitment handles.

STEP 2: Simulate & Scale

Our customized AI models (BioFusionDTI & TPCA-LLM) ingest native structural, perturbation and interaction maps with sequence profiles to predict intervention points and corresponding target-ligand interactions.

STEP 3: Active Proximity

We link target disease proteins to native cellular machinery using our Mapping Stage recruitment handles, creating chemical chimeras that act as matchmakers to degrade, edit, or relocate them.

ACT 3 Arsenal Platforms

Our Established Mapping Platforms

To strategically intervene in the cell, we first built next-generation biophysical and machine learning tools to profile targets and map macromolecular complex dynamics without chemical modifications. As the inventors of Thermal Proximity Co-Aggregation (TPCA)[1]—a system-wide approach based on CETSA-MS or TPP to profile protein complexes in intact cells—our laboratory has focused on continually improvising and deploying TPP and TPCA to dissect the target-binding and downstream mechanisms of bioactive compounds.

🔬 Protein Biophysics & Chemo-Proteomics Toolkits

High-Throughput Target & Ligand Profiling

MAPS-iTSA Permutation Profiling

Applies genetic binary sensing matrices to pool multiple compounds in optimized permutations. Deconvolutes thousands of target binders simultaneously via non-negative LASSO regression.

Advantages & Impact: Completely bypasses single-compound throughput limits, maximizing mass spectrometer instrument utilization and resolving hundreds of hits in record time.
Ref: Ji et al., Cell Chem Biol 2023 [2]
Microscale & Clinical Sample Profiling

STASIS

Scales thermal profiling requirements down from 100 µg to as little as 1 µg of protein. Integrates single-tube zero-transfer thermal heating and functionalized spintip digestion (SISPROT).

Advantages & Impact: Protects minute patient samples and rare primary cells from peptide loss during transfer, enabling target deconvolution directly in clinical biopsies.
Ref: Lu et al., Anal Chem 2023 [3]
Label-Free Proteomics

SIP-TPP

Introduces an unheated "spike-in proteome" (SIP) to rescue thermally denatured and depleted proteins during label-free Data-Independent Acquisition (DIA).

Advantages & Impact: Boosts analytical peptide coverage by 17.6% and detects 30% more proteins under label-free DIA quantitation, preventing data gaps.
Ref: Wang et al., Anal Chem 2024 [4]
Spatial & Complex Assembly Monitoring

Slim-TPCA

Condenses traditional 10-temperature melting profiles down to 3 or 4 optimized points. Replaces bootstrap simulations with Beta distribution modeling.

Advantages & Impact: Boosts experimental throughput over 3-fold, eliminates batch effects, and achieves an incredible 100-fold computational acceleration.
Ref: Sun et al., Nat Commun 2023 [5]
Organelle-Specific Dynamics

Mito-TPCA

Combines localized proximity-labeling enzymes with thermal co-aggregation to map organelle-specific protein-protein interactions directly in living cells.

Advantages & Impact: Captures spatial complex dynamics inside organelles, resolving mitochondrial subcomplex disassembly during cellular events like autophagy.
Ref: Zhang et al., Cell Comm Sign 2026 [6]
Tag-Free Structural Conformational Mapping

RAPID-OPA

Utilizes *ortho*-phthalaldehyde (OPA) reaction kinetics and "inverse detection" of tryptic peptide depletion to paint exposed lysine residues inside intact cells.

Advantages & Impact: Resolves exact pocket binding residues and drug-induced structural remodeling directly within intact cells, without requiring engineered tags.
Ref: Zheng et al., Chem Sci 2024 [7]

🔒 Unlocking the Dark Proteome

Discovering previously hidden cellular players to construct novel molecular warheads.

🔒

Molecular Sieve Discovery Core

Standard drug discovery misses hundreds of tiny proteins (the "dark citizens" of the cell). To identify these unknown players and expand our chemical toolkit, we design magnetic core-shell covalent organic frameworks (COFs) featuring uniform 2.72 nm pore grids.

These advanced physical sieves combine size exclusion with acid precipitation to selectively enrich and identify short open reading frame (sORF)-encoded microproteins. We successfully identified 195 unique microproteins during dynamic cell death events, adding entirely new targets and recruitment coordinates to our chemical matchmaking library.

Ref: Li et al., Anal Chem 2026 / Zhang et al., APSB 2026

💻 AI & Contextual Network-Mining

CPI Virtual Screening

BioFusionDTI

A multimodal deep learning architecture that fuses drug GCN graphs with ESM-2 protein sequence embeddings via a Bilinear Attention Network (BAN).

Advantages & Impact: Eliminates "cold-start" prediction blockades and outputs clear, single-residue binding resolution maps.
Ref: Chen et al., JCIM 2025
Complex Dynamics

Perturbed TPCA-LLM

A specialized Graph Transformer pairing sequence profiles with dynamic physical co-behavior matrices from Slim-TPCA and Mito-TPCA runs.

Advantages & Impact: Predicts transient, low-affinity, or highly context-specific macromolecular interface remodeling.
Model: In-house LLM
Network Mining

Contextual Network-Mining

Exploits public and in-house experimental perturbation datasets (CRISPR/Drug sensitivity) to filter biological noise and map cell-specific assemblies.

Advantages & Impact: Identifies contextual essential genes and dynamic relationships, providing the exact coordinates of cellular network rewiring.
Ref: Zeng et al., Knowl-Based Syst 2026
ACT 4 The Next Phase Tinkering

New Endeavours

By leveraging physical target engagement maps and native, derivatization-free ligands developed in the Mapping Stage as modular "recruitment handles," we design multifunctional chemical chimeras. These act as molecular matchmakers to force key players in this cellular metropolis to gather and crystallize specific therapeutic outcomes.

The Proximity Chimera Toolkit

Using Mapping Stage interaction networks as guides and native ligands as physical building blocks to engineer chemical matchmakers.

🗑️

PROTAC Degraders

Chemically tethers a disease-causing target protein directly to native E3 ubiquitin ligases (CRBN, VHL), triggering selective, proteasome-mediated demolition.

🧹

LYTACs, ATACs & AUTACs

Recruits extracellular receptors or lysosomal/autophagic machinery to sweep large pathogenic aggregates or entire dysfunctional organelle neighborhoods (e.g. damaged mitochondria) out of the city.

✒️

PhosTAC & KineTAC Editors

Tethers kinases or phosphatases directly to non-native substrates to dynamically edit post-translational modifications (PTMs), bypassing upstream signal blockades.

🔒

DUBTAC Stabilizers

Tethers specific targets directly to deubiquitinases (such as OTUB1) to rescue essential protective enzymes from premature degradation and maintain homeostasis.

🌱 Natural Products

Targeting Psoriasis via ACLY Metabolic Reprogramming [10]

Applying the MAPS-iTSA platform to screen a panel of 50 structurally diverse natural products identified that Demethylzeylasteral (derived from the medicinal herb Tripterygium wilfordii) directly binds and inhibits ATP-citrate lyase (ACLY). This target engagement halts lipid metabolic reprogramming in keratinocytes, successfully alleviating skin inflammation in imiquimod (IMQ)-induced psoriasiform mouse models.

🔬 Mechanistic Pathways

Veratramine Autophagy Flux Blockade [6]

Integrated TPP-TPCA profiling revealed that the plant natural product veratramine (VAM) targets the ATP6V1C1 subunit of vacuolar-type ATPase (V-ATPase). This binding drives the dissociation of the proton pump, disabling lysosomal acidification and shutting down autophagic survival mechanisms to suppress pancreatic cancer progression.

🧬 Fluorescence Imaging

Deconvoluting Liver Cancer Probes hLR and cLG [8]

By deconvoluting the precise cellular targets of liver-cancer-specific fluorescent probes (identifying direct membrane targets SMPD1 and SLC27A2), we provided the deep biophysical validation necessary to support real-world, fluorescence-guided clinical tumor resections.

🔋 Mitochondrial Metabolism

Elesclomol Dissociation of the PDKs-PDH Complex [9]

Using organelle-specific Mito-TPCA, we revealed that the copper ionophore drug elesclomol selectively induces iron- and copper-dependent cuproptosis by physically disrupting the binding of pyruvate dehydrogenase kinases (PDKs) to the PDH complex in mitochondria, driving selective tumor death.

🦠 Pathogen Metabolites

PQS Bacterial Metabolite Hijacks CNMT driving Severe Macrophage Ferroptosis [7]

We discovered that the Pseudomonas aeruginosa quorum-sensing metabolite PQS directly targets the host methyltransferase CNMT, hyperactivating the histidine methylation of transferrin receptor-1 (TFR1). This molecular hijack results in severe macrophage iron overload and ferroptosis during severe respiratory infections.

[1] TPCA (Thermal Proximity Co-Aggregation)

Thermal proximity coaggregation for system-wide profiling of protein complex dynamics in cells

Tan, C. S. H., Go, K. D., Bisteau, X., Dai, L., Yong, C. H., Prabhu, N., Ozturk, M. B., Lim, Y. T., Sreekumar, L., Lengqvist, J., Tergaonkar, V., Kaldis, P., Sobota, R. M., & Nordlund, P. (2018). Science, 359(6380), 1170–1177.

[3] STASIS (Microscale Sampling)

Scaled-Down Thermal Profiling and Coaggregation Analysis of the Proteome for Drug Target and Protein Interaction Analysis

[4] SIP-TPP (Label-Free Proteomics)

Spike-In Proteome Enhances Data-Independent Acquisition for Thermal Proteome Profiling

Key Contribution: Introduced the SIP(Spike-in Proteome)-TPP strategy for DIA-TPP, adding a non-heated proteome to prevent thermal denaturation depletion, boosting proteome coverage by 17.6% and detecting 30% more proteins under label-free DIA quantitation.

[5] Slim-TPCA

Improved in situ characterization of protein complex dynamics at scale with thermal proximity co-aggregation

Sun, S., Zheng, Z., Wang, J., Li, F., He, A., opacity, K., Zhang, S., Lu, J. H., Tian, R., & Tan, C. S. H. (2023). Nature Communications, 14(1), 7697.

[7] Host-Pathogen Ferroptosis

Key Contribution: Identified host methyltransferase CNMT as the direct intracellular receptor of bacterial QS metabolite PQS. Binding of PQS enhances CNMT His MTase activity, catalyzing methylation of TFR1 at His35 to upregulate protein expression, driving macrophage iron overload and lipid peroxidation cascades during severe lung infections.

[8] Complementary Tumor Imaging

Key Contribution: Developed cLG (cancerous Liver Green) and hLR (healthy Liver Red) probes for complementary boundary imaging of hepatocellular carcinoma. Identified fatty acid transporter SLC27A2 (FATP2) as the gating importer of cLG via CRISPRa activation screen, and mapped SMPD1 as hLR's membrane target via thermal proteome profiling (TPP).

[9] Suborganelle Complex Dynamics

[10] AI-Chemoproteomic Deconvolution

Thermal Proteomics and AI-assisted Target Deconvolution Identify ACLY as a Direct Target of Demethylzeylasteral in Psoriasis

Key Contribution: Developed an integrated target discovery framework combining MAPS-iTSA biophysical screening with structure-aware HoloGNN Graph Neural Networks to identify ACLY as the direct functional receptor of medicinal triterpenoid Demethylzeylasteral (with low-micromolar affinity). Proved that inhibiting ACLY reverses lipogenic SREBP-dependent reprogramming in keratinocytes to resolve psoriasiform dermatitis.
